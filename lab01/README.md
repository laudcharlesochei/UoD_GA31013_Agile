# Practical Manual

## Lab 02.1: Standing Up a New Scrum Team

---

## Lab Overview

This **lab** puts you in the position of an internal Agile coach who has just been asked to help a newly-formed team adopt Scrum from a standing start. It is built on the **case study from Lesson 2 — Scrum Framework I: Roles, Artifacts and Events** and is designed to be worked through *after* you have covered Scrum theory, the three accountabilities, the three artifacts and their commitments, and the five Scrum events.

The lab is **formative and discussion-based**. There is no single "correct" answer — you are assessed on how clearly you apply Scrum Guide terminology and reasoning to a messy, realistic situation, not on reciting definitions. It is designed to prepare you for exactly the kind of ambiguous, real-world request you are likely to meet on placement or in your apprenticeship employer.

**Format:** Individual preparation, then small-group work, then a whole-class share-out.
**Total time:** 45–55 minutes (can be split across two shorter sessions if needed).

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

- Identify who on a newly-formed team is best placed to hold the **Product Owner** accountability, and explain what decision-making authority they need.
- Specify the **first artifact** a new Scrum Team needs before it can hold a meaningful Sprint Planning session, and describe how to create it from nothing.
- Design a **Sprint cadence and event schedule** for a team that has never run Scrum before.
- Construct a short, evidence-based argument for replacing a legacy reporting habit (a weekly status e-mail) with a Scrum event, addressing stakeholders' underlying concerns rather than dismissing them.
- Apply Scrum terminology (Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done, Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog) accurately and consistently in a written recommendation.

---

## Prerequisites

**Before beginning, make sure you can confidently explain:**

- The three Scrum accountabilities — **Product Owner**, **Scrum Master**, **Developers** — and what each is accountable for.
- The three Scrum artifacts — **Product Backlog**, **Sprint Backlog**, **Increment** — and their associated commitments — **Product Goal**, **Sprint Goal**, **Definition of Done**.
- The five Scrum events — **the Sprint**, **Sprint Planning**, **Daily Scrum**, **Sprint Review**, **Sprint Retrospective** — and the purpose of each.
- Scrum's three pillars of empiricism: **transparency**, **inspection**, **adaptation**.

If any of these feel shaky, revisit Lesson 2, sections 2.1–2.4, before starting this lab.

**You will need:**

- A copy of the scenario below (or access to it on the VLE).
- A notebook, shared document, or whiteboard for your group's working notes.
- Optional: sticky notes or a digital board (e.g. a spreadsheet, Trello, or Miro) to sketch a first Product Backlog.

---

## The Scenario

> **Standing Up a New Scrum Team**
>
> A logistics company is building a driver-tracking mobile app. Leadership asks you to help "set up Scrum" for a newly-formed team of six developers, a designer and a product manager. There is currently no backlog, no defined Sprint length, and stakeholders expect weekly status e-mails.

Read the scenario twice before moving on. Notice what is **missing** (a backlog, a Sprint length, an agreed way of working) as well as what already **exists** (a cross-functional group of people, a business need, and a stakeholder expectation you will need to manage, not ignore).

---

## Part 1 (Individual — 10 minutes)

### Clarify the Starting Conditions

Before discussing the scenario as a group, work alone for 10 minutes and note down your own answers to these framing questions. Bring your notes into Part 2.

1. List every **role mentioned** in the scenario (six developers, a designer, a product manager) and, next to each, write which **Scrum accountability** you think they map to — or "unclear, needs deciding."
2. In one sentence, write down **why** you think there is currently no backlog and no Sprint length. (There is no right answer here — this is about surfacing your assumptions before you compare them with your group.)
3. Note **one risk** of leaving the weekly status e-mail habit in place unchanged, and **one risk** of removing it too abruptly.

**Deliverable from Part 1:** a short set of individual notes — no formal write-up needed yet.

---

## Part 2 (Small Group — 20–25 minutes)

### Work the Discussion Questions

Form groups of 3–4. Compare your Part 1 notes first (5 minutes), then work through the three discussion questions below as a group, in order. Allocate roughly 5–7 minutes per question and appoint one person to capture the group's answer in writing.

#### Discussion Question 1 — Product Ownership (≈7 minutes)

> Who on this team is best placed to become Product Owner, and what must they be given authority over?

Work through this in two steps:

1. **Candidate check.** Of the people mentioned (developers, designer, product manager), who has the closest, most direct view of customer and business value? Justify your choice against the Scrum Guide's description of the Product Owner as accountable for **maximising the value of the product** resulting from the team's work.
2. **Authority check.** A title alone does not make someone an effective Product Owner. List at least **three concrete decisions** this person must be trusted to make unilaterally (for example: backlog order, what counts as "good enough" for a Sprint Review, whether to accept or reject a stakeholder's mid-Sprint request) for the role to function.

#### Discussion Question 2 — The First Artifact (≈7 minutes)

> What is the very first artifact the team needs before it can hold a meaningful Sprint Planning session?

1. Name the artifact and its associated commitment.
2. Describe, in practical terms, **how you would create a first version of it in one afternoon** with this team — who is in the room, what questions you ask, and what "good enough to start" looks like (it does not need to be complete or perfectly ordered on day one).
3. Sketch **five to eight illustrative Product Backlog items** for the driver-tracking app (one line each is enough — for example, "As a dispatcher, I can see a driver's live location on a map"). This is practice for writing real backlog items, not a graded deliverable.

#### Discussion Question 3 — Managing the Stakeholder Habit (≈7 minutes)

> How would you persuade stakeholders that a Sprint Review replaces the weekly status e-mail — without appearing to reduce visibility?

1. Identify the **underlying need** behind the weekly e-mail (visibility, reassurance, early warning of slippage) — the habit itself is not the real problem to solve.
2. Explain **how a Sprint Review, done well, meets that same underlying need** — and where it falls short (for example: it is periodic, not weekly, and does not cover items that are not yet done).
3. Propose **one lightweight bridge** the team could offer during the transition (for example: a short async written summary alongside the first two or three Sprint Reviews) so stakeholders are not asked to give up something for nothing. Be specific about what it contains and how long it takes to produce.

**Deliverable from Part 2:** one shared document or page per group, containing a clear written answer to all three questions.

---

## Part 3 (Small Group — 10–15 minutes)

### Draft a "First 30 Days" Plan

Using your answers from Part 2, produce a short, practical plan for this team's first month, covering:

| Week | Focus | Key activity |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Foundations | Confirm accountabilities; run a backlog-seeding workshop; agree Sprint length |
| Week 2 | First Sprint | Hold Sprint Planning; set a Sprint Goal; begin Daily Scrums |
| Week 3 | Mid-Sprint discipline | Continue Daily Scrums; protect the Sprint Goal from scope creep |
| Week 4 | Close the loop | Hold Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective; replace/adapt the status e-mail |

For each week, add **one sentence of your own** explaining the single most important thing that must happen for the plan to succeed. Keep it concrete — name a person, an artifact, or an event, not a generality.

**Deliverable from Part 3:** the completed table with your group's added detail.

---

## Whole-Class Share-Out (5–10 minutes)

Each group nominates one spokesperson to share **one decision** from their plan that they expect other groups to disagree with. As a class, briefly discuss where opinions diverged and why — there is genuine, defensible disagreement in Agile practice, and surfacing it is more valuable than pretending there is one right answer.

---

## What You Submit

1. Your Part 1 individual notes (brief — a few lines is fine).
2. Your group's written answers to the three Discussion Questions (Part 2).
3. Your group's completed "First 30 Days" table (Part 3).

Submit as a single shared document, exported PDF, or a link to your shared workspace, per your seminar tutor's instructions.

---

## Reflection Prompts (individual, 100–150 words)

Answer **one** of the following after the session, in your own words:

- Which of the three discussion questions did your group find hardest to agree on, and why?
- If you were the actual Product Owner in this scenario, what is the first decision you would make in week one, and what would you need from the Scrum Master to make it well?
- Compare this scenario to a real or placement workplace you know. What would be *harder* to change there than in this idealised case study, and why?

## Facilitator Notes (optional — for tutors running this session)

<details>
<summary>Click to expand facilitator guidance</summary>

- **Common sticking point:** groups often default to making the most senior developer the Product Owner. Use this as a teaching moment — technical seniority and product/business-value judgement are different skills, and the Product Owner does not need to be technical.
- **Common sticking point:** the "first artifact" answer is the Product Backlog (with an initial, even rough, Product Goal) — some groups will answer "a Sprint Backlog" or "a project plan." Redirect them to the artifact/commitment pairing covered in Lesson 2, section 2.3.
- **Timing:** if running short on time, Part 3 (the 30-day plan) can be set as homework instead of in-session group work.
- **Extension activity:** ask faster groups to also draft the single Daily Scrum question they would ask this team on day one of Sprint 1.

</details>
